Node velocity treatment as reported by secondaries citing Holland:

- Velocity field v = ∇S/m; quantum potential Q = −ħ²/(2mR) ∇²R; guidance law
  dx/dt = (ħ/m) Im(∇Ψ/Ψ) = ∇S/m. Equation labels Eqs.(6)/(7)/(10) in
  de Gosson–Hiley, Phys. Lett. A short-time propagator paper (2013)
  (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3820027/ and
  https://eprints.bbk.ac.uk/id/eprint/8102/7/8102.pdf); those authors cite Holland
  [7,8] (Nuovo Cimento B 116 (2001) 1043–1070; 1143–1172) for the Hamiltonian
  trajectory reading. Treat those Eq. numbers as de Gosson–Hiley labels, NOT as
  Holland book equation numbers.
- Non-crossing rule restated in the same source: "The Bohmian equations of motion
  are a priori only defined when R≠0 (that is, outside the nodes of the wave
  function)" and "An important feature of the quantum trajectories ... is that they
  cannot cross". Modern normalizing-flow restatement cites the same Holland
  section: "trajectories can-not cross or pass through nodes [14, §3.3.8]"
  (arXiv:2604.25137, § citing Holland book, p.2, Eq.(5) dx/dt=v, m dv/dt=−∇(V+Q)).
- Independent trajectory-methods review (Wyatt/Bittner, quant-ph/0302088, p.2):
  "The quantum trajectories obey two important noncrossing rules: (1) they cannot
  cross nodal surfaces (along which ψ=0); (2) they cannot cross each other."
  Same review (p.7–9): "the quantum potential becomes large around wavefunction
  nodes and quasinodes", "Near nodes ... propagation of Bohmian trajectories
  becomes problematic ... trajectories tend to avoid nodal regions ... under-sampling".
  Cited as numerical-behaviour note, not as a Holland core-cutoff prescription.

1D HO-1 (first-excited oscillator) stationary-node treatment via Holland-lineage
secondaries:

- Holland & Kyprianidis, Ann. Inst. Henri Poincaré 49(3) (1988), "Quantum
  potential, uncertainty and the classical limit"
  (https://www.numdam.org/item/AIHPA_1988__49_3_325_0.pdf), §3 "STATIONARY STATES",
  p.328: "since the energy eigenstates are often real functions, the associated
  velocity field is often zero. The particle is at rest where one would classically
  expect it to move since the quantum force (−∇Q) cancels the classical force
  (−∇V)." Same section, particle-in-a-box example p.330: spatial part is real so
  "v = ∇S/m = 0. The energy entirely resides in quantum potential energy". This is
  the real-stationary-state rule that covers 1D HO-1 (real eigenfunction, S=−Et,
  ∇S=0 away from node); the paper does not single out HO-1 by name on the pages
  excerpted.
- R. Dabin, Imperial College MSc dissertation (2009),
  "De Broglie-Bohm Theory: A Hidden Variables Approach"
  (https://www.imperial.ac.uk/media/imperial-college/research-centres-and-groups/theoretical-physics/msc/dissertations/2009/Robert-Dabin-Dissertation.pdf),
  p.13 (§2.2.3 Stationary States, citing Holland (1993) pp.158–165 and pp.33–34):
  "if the energy eigenfunction is real then S=−Ent and so does not depend on
  position. This means that the velocity field is zero, ∇S/m=0 and so the particle
  is at rest" and explicitly "In the case of the harmonic oscillator case the
  wavefunction of every state ψn has the gradient of its phase equal to zero and so
  the actual particle is before measurement motionless."
- M. Lawyer, BYU senior thesis (2020), "Bohmian Mechanical Trajectories for the
  Simple Harmonic Oscillator"
  (https://physics.byu.edu/docs/thesis/1459): "The trajectories corresponding to
  basis states exhibit no particle motion — a particle in any basis state is
  stationary" (Ch.2 discussion of HO eigenstates); non-crossing proof in
  Appendix B, pp.39–40 ("Bohmian mechanical trajectories cannot cross ... often
  referred to as the hydrodynamic interpretation ... additional discussion ...
  Peter Holland [11]"). Thesis cites Holland book as Ref.[11].

## 3. Core cutoff — explicit negative

- No finite vortex-core radius, no healing-length-type regulator, no truncated-Q
  cap, and no "core cutoff" parameter attributed to Holland (1993) or to
  Bohm–Hiley (1993) was found in this sweep.
- What the sources do instead: exclude nodes from the domain (R≠0 / ψ≠0), let
  Q = −ħ²/2m (∇²R/R) diverge (R⁻¹∇²R→∞ on Wikipedia de Broglie–Bohm summary;
  "infinitely repulsive" in Dennis et al. 2014; "becomes large around nodes" in
  Wyatt/Bittner quant-ph/0302088), keep v=∇S/m single-valued so integral curves
  cannot cross or pass through a node (Holland §3.3.8, p.85; de Gosson–Hiley 2013;
  arXiv:2604.25137 citing §3.3.8).
- Numerical-trajectory literature notes the practical consequence (under-sampling
  near nodes, large Q, derivative-evaluation difficulty) but the fixes discussed
  there (adaptive grids, equidistribution, cluster modelling in Wyatt/Bittner
  quant-ph/0302088; energy-conserving Q approximations in Garashchuk–Rassolov
  lineage) are later computational devices, not a Bohm–Hiley/Holland core cutoff.
- 1D HO-1 specific: no separate "HO-1 node equation" with a core radius was found
  in the Holland/Bohm–Hiley material swept; the HO-1 case falls under the general
  real-stationary-state rule (v=0 off-node, undefined exactly at R=0).
