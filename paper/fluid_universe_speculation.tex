\begin{abstract}
This is a status report on a speculative ultraviolet completion in which the
vacuum is a continuous nematic director field and particles are topological
defects. The structural checks below pass their algebra and scaling gates; no dynamical UV claim does.
The only defensible statements about the UV dynamics are negative: a nematic director has target
space $RP^2$ with $\pi_1=Z_2$, $\pi_2=Z$, $\pi_3=Z$, so a three-fold sector is
not automatic; the $\cos3\phi$ term used elsewhere is inserted by hand; and the
naive rotor-to-circulant map cannot produce three distinct lepton masses
because the $k=1$ and $k=2$ sectors are degenerate. We list the tests required
before any positive claim can be made.
\end{abstract}

\section{What is assumed, not derived}
\label{sec:assumed}

\emph{Speculation.} Take a real unit-vector (director) field $n(x)$ with
Frank-Oseen free energy
\begin{equation}
E_{\rm FO}=\frac12\int d^3x\left[
K_1(\nabla\cdot n)^2+K_2(n\cdot\nabla\times n)^2
+K_3|n\times(\nabla\times n)|^2\right].
\label{eq:fo}
\end{equation}
The one-constant case $K_1=K_2=K_3$ linearises to $\omega^2=c^2k^2$ for
transverse director modes. \emph{This is not an emergent Lorentz invariance
claim.} It is one mode's dispersion in a medium with a preferred frame, and the
mode count, polarisations, and coupling to matter have not been computed.

\section{What the structural checks establish}
\label{sec:structural}

\emph{Derived.} For the quadratic Frank-Oseen energy with background
$n_0=z$ and transverse components $(a,b)$,
\begin{equation}
M=\begin{pmatrix}
K_1k_x^2+K_2k_y^2+K_3k_z^2 & (K_1-K_2)k_xk_y\\
(K_1-K_2)k_xk_y & K_1k_y^2+K_2k_x^2+K_3k_z^2
\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
For $K_1=K_2=K_3=K$, $M=Kk^2 I$: two degenerate transverse modes with
$\omega^2=(K/\rho)k^2$. There is no longitudinal propagating mode, no gauge
field or vector potential, and therefore no photon candidate. This is a
statement about one dispersion relation; it is not emergent Lorentz
invariance, because the background $n_0$ selects a preferred frame.

\emph{Derived.} For the exact energy $E=E_2+E_4+V$ with a quartic Skyrme term
and a non-negative potential, scaling $x\to\lambda x$ gives
\begin{equation}
E(\lambda)=\lambda^{d-2}E_2+\lambda^{d-4}E_4+\lambda^d V.
\end{equation}
At $d=3$, a stationary soliton satisfies $E_2-E_4+3V=0$, and the second
derivative is $2(E_4+3V)>0$. The necessary region for a stable scaling
soliton is $K>0$, $\kappa>0$, $\mu\ge0$; $\kappa=0$ has no stationary point,
and $\kappa<0$ is unbounded below. This is not an existence proof. No Hopfion
solution is claimed.

For a configuration connecting adjacent vacua at fixed $y$,
$E_{2D}\ge T L_y$, with equality only when $\partial_y\phi=0$; this is a
trivial positivity bound, not an exact dimensional reduction. The effective
moment of inertia is $I_{\rm eff}=2\pi\int\rho(r)r^3dr$. For a $1/r^4$ tail
it is logarithmically divergent; a finite $I_{\rm eff}$ requires a tail
steeper than $r^{-4}$. The reduction used in the original sketch is therefore
not finite.

\emph{Numeric (1D only).} The 1D sine-Gordon core passes its validation
baselines: the kink energy converges to $T=(8/3)\sqrt{\mu}$ at observed order
two, the analytic force matches finite differences below $10^{-6}$, gradient
flow decreases the energy monotonically, and the rotor Fourier and
finite-difference spectra agree below $10^{-3}$. The Hessian is symmetric and
stable, but its zero-mode count is only partial because Dirichlet boundaries
lift the translation. No 3D lattice, Hopf charge, or Z3 order run exists, so
claim 15 is not achieved.

\emph{Numeric (Hopf estimator).} For the analytic $H=1$ map, the preimage
linking estimator gives $H=0.999331$, $0.999833$, and $0.999958$ at 100, 200,
and 400 samples; reflection flips the sign, and rotation and shear leave it
unchanged. The normalized grid Chern-Simons estimator (spectral derivatives,
smooth compactification, convention $-1/(16\pi^2)$) gives $1.000141$,
$0.9999998$, and $1.00000004$ at $N=32,48,64$. The two methods agree, so
baseline 4 passes. The grid estimator needs $N\ge32$ for $10^{-3}$ accuracy on
the analytic map; $N=16$ and $24$ are under-resolved. This is why the $N=32$
3D pilot's $H$ values are only indicative.

\emph{Numeric (static, not a simulation).} The analytic $H=1$ map has finite
Faddeev-Niemi energies: $E_2=233.635$ and $E_4=159.262$ at $N=64$, with
$H=1.000000$. The Derrick scaling minimum is at $\lambda^*=0.82563$, where the
virial is satisfied by construction. This is a finite-energy $H=1$
configuration, not a stationary solution under non-scale deformations, not a
stability proof, and not a Z3-order measurement. A single-seed $N=32$ pilot
with the full $E_2+E_4+Z_3$ energy is not converged: at 1200 steps the force
RMS is still $0.10$, and the Hopf charge and azimuthal order parameter change
with step count and resolution. Diagnostic values at $\mu=0,1,5,20$ are
$H=0.74976,0.69989,0.38079,0.05237$ and
$Z_3=0,0.0930,0.4356,0.9602$, but an $N=64$ spot check differs. No coexistence
conclusion is drawn. A targeted $N=64$ diagnostic (run separately with `make n64`)
resolves the trend better. In the pure Faddeev-Niemi case the flow reaches a
near-stationary $H=1$ candidate, but once the Z3 potential is switched on,
higher azimuthal order is accompanied by a lower Hopf charge. The run is still
not converged, so it is not a production result and no coexistence is claimed.
A damped scale iteration in the pure Faddeev-Niemi case reaches a near-virial-free
$H=1$ candidate. Its Hessian stability is not established: a matrix-free
Hessian check failed a dense validation, so no stability conclusion is drawn.
No stable Hopfion is demonstrated.

\paragraph{Residual anisotropy and Lorentz bounds.} The two transverse
director speeds are equal only when $K_1=K_2=K_3$. Define
\begin{equation}
\xi=\frac{c_{\max}^2-c_{\min}^2}{\overline{c^2}}.
\end{equation}
At tree level $\xi=0$ for equal $K$, while $K=(1,2,3)$ gives $\xi=1.00348$ over
4000 sampled directions. Hence there is no emergent metric and no
Lorentz-invariant IR point at tree level; the background $n_0$ selects a
preferred frame.

The director modes are neutral. Photon birefringence, GRB dispersion, CMB
isotropy, and clock/cavity limits therefore do not directly constrain them.
For orientation, the relevant orders are $10^{-34}$ (birefringence
coefficient), $10^{-18}$ (GRB linear dispersion at 10 GeV), $10^{-5}$ (CMB
isotropy), and $10^{-17}$ (clock/cavity); these are order-of-magnitude
estimates, not calibrated bounds on this model. A conditional statement is
possible: if a model identified the director modes with photons with O(1)
coupling, equal $K$ would pass every $\Delta c/c$ bound exactly, whereas
$\xi=1.00348$ would be excluded by many orders of magnitude. That identification
is not made here.

\section{Known topological obstruction}
\label{sec:topology}

A nematic director is headless: $n\sim-n$, so the order-parameter space is
$RP^2$. Its homotopy groups are
\begin{equation}
\pi_1(RP^2)=Z_2,\qquad \pi_2(RP^2)=Z,\qquad \pi_3(RP^2)=Z.
\label{eq:pi}
\end{equation}
There is no three-fold factor. The direct claim that nematic topology
produces $Z_3$ is therefore falsified. We checked the three standard
extensions: a rank-three order parameter, three coupled nematics, and a
three-fold lattice. Each requires an explicit three-fold structure; none
derives $Z_3$ from $RP^2$ alone. The $\cos3\phi$ term is inserted by hand.
A different, specified extension is not excluded, but it would have to be
constructed and tested explicitly.

\section{The rotor map fails on degeneracy}
\label{sec:rotor}

\emph{Derived (conditional).} For a quantum rotor
\begin{equation}
H=\frac{L^2}{2I}+\mu\left(1-\cos3\phi\right),
\label{eq:rotor}
\end{equation}
the potential is even in $\phi$ and invariant under $\phi\to-\phi$. In the
Fourier basis $n=k+3j$, the sectors $k=1$ and $k=2$ are therefore degenerate:
$E_1=E_2$. A map that assigns one eigenvalue per sector,
$a=(\ell_0+2\ell_1)/3$, $c=(\ell_1-\ell_0)/3$, produces at most two distinct
values and cannot reproduce three charged-lepton masses. This is a structural
failure of the naive map, not a numerical accident. Exact diagonalisation
confirms it: at $I=\mu=1$, $E_0=0.891186$ and $E_1=E_2=1.317035$, so the map
gives only two distinct roots. The naive rotor route is dropped.

\section{Required tests before any positive claim}
\label{sec:tests}

\begin{enumerate}
\item A validated Hopf-charge estimator on an analytic $H=1$ configuration,
using two independent methods, with convergence order in $N$.
\item A numerical Hopfion solution, since Derrick scaling is only a necessary
condition.
\item A one-loop analysis of anisotropy parameters, or an explicit downgrade of
any emergent-metric claim. No physical calibration of the $K_i$ or the UV
scale exists, so no quantitative Lorentz-violation bound is possible yet.
\item A 3D run achieving $|H|\ge1$ \emph{and} $Z_3$ order with at least ten
random seeds. No such run exists. Earlier lost-run values are not cited.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The nematic-fluid picture currently supplies a vocabulary, not a calculation.
It is kept separate from Paper~A so that the $Z_3$/Koide/RG analysis stands or
falls on its own. On present evidence the fluid-universe track has no
falsifiable prediction and no validated simulation.
